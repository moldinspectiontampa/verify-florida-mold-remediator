# How to Verify a Florida Mold Remediator Before You Hire One

Tampa homeowners deal with mold more often than homeowners in most other Florida cities. Warm air most of the year, heavy afternoon storms, high humidity, and air conditioning running almost all year mean moisture problems show up often, and mold follows moisture. When mold shows up in a Tampa home, the first decision is who to hire, and Florida gives every homeowner tools to check that decision before money changes hands.

Hiring the wrong company for mold remediation in Tampa FL is not like hiring the wrong painter. The wrong choice can delay the real fix, leave the moisture source in place, or make the problem worse. The checks below take little time, and they protect a homeowner far better than a sales pitch ever will.

## Check the License First at MyFloridaLicense.com

Florida licenses mold professionals through the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, known as the DBPR. There are two separate license types: mold assessor and mold remediator. The assessor inspects and tests. The remediator removes mold and dries the area. They are different licenses for a reason, and that reason is covered in the next section.

Before you hire anyone for mold removal in Tampa FL, look the company up at MyFloridaLicense.com. Search the company name and the name of the person who will do the work. Check three things: the license is active, the license covers the work being offered, and the name on the license matches the company in front of you.

If no license shows up, or the license belongs to a different name or company, stop and ask why. A legitimate company expects this question and answers it plainly. A company that gets annoyed by a license question has already told you something important.

## Keep the Assessor and the Remediator Separate

Florida law keeps mold assessment and mold remediation apart. Under Florida Statute 468.8419, the provider who assesses a property is not the provider who performs the remediation on that same property. The person who finds the problem should not be the same person who sells the fix, because the person who finds the problem has a financial reason to find a bigger one.

This separation is the single most important protection a homeowner has when hiring for mold remediation in Tampa FL. An independent assessment comes first. The remediation plan follows what the assessment found. After the work is done, clearance testing should come from an independent assessor, not from the company that did the cleanup.

If one company offers to inspect, test, clean up, and clear its own work on the same job, that is a warning sign, not a convenience. Walk away and find providers who keep the roles apart.

## Ask How They Find the Moisture Source

Mold is a moisture problem first and a cleaning problem second. A company that talks only about removing mold, and never about finding the water, is skipping the step that decides whether the mold comes back.

Ask these questions before you agree to anything: how do you find the source of the moisture, what readings do you take, and how do you prove the area is dry when the work is done? A serious provider checks moisture levels with a meter, looks at the usual Tampa failure points, and explains the drying plan in plain language.

In Tampa homes, the usual failure points are a clogged air conditioning condensate drain, a small roof leak over an attic, a slow plumbing leak inside a wall, and storm-driven rain getting past stucco or window seals. Standing water and soaked materials must be handled first, because no cleanup means anything while the area is still wet. That is why water extraction and mitigation comes before removal work in a proper plan: get the water out, dry the materials, and only then deal with what grew in the damp.

If a company cannot explain how it finds the source and proves the area dry, it has not earned the job.

## Get the Plan in Writing Before You Agree

Before any mold remediation work starts, get a written plan. It should state which areas will be worked on, what will be removed, what will be cleaned, how the work area will be contained, how drying will be done, and what happens after the work is finished. It should also state who actually does the work, whether any part is handed to a subcontractor, and who is responsible if something goes wrong.

Verbal promises are easy to forget and hard to prove. A written plan protects both sides, and a company that refuses to put its plan in writing is telling you how it handles problems later.

Ask about clearance testing in the same conversation. After remediation, an independent assessor should check the area and confirm the work is complete. Build that step into the plan from the start, so there is no argument about it later.

## Five Warning Signs That Mean Slow Down

1. Pressure to start the same day, with a reason that expires if you wait.
2. One company offering to inspect, test, and clean up the same job.
3. No license that you can verify yourself at MyFloridaLicense.com.
4. No written plan, only a verbal price and a handshake.
5. Full payment demanded before any work starts.

Any one of these is a reason to slow down and get a second opinion from an independent provider. Two of them together is a reason to stop completely.

## Tampa Homes Have Their Own Mold Patterns

The rules for mold remediation are statewide, but Tampa homes fail in local ways. Older homes near the water, newer homes with complex air conditioning systems, condos, and rentals each get mold for different reasons. Humidity stays high for much of the year, summer storms push rain into places it should not go, and air conditioning runs long enough that a small drain clog can keep a closet damp for weeks without anyone noticing.

This guidance applies to homes across Tampa, including South Tampa, Davis Islands, Ybor City, Hyde Park, and Seminole Heights. After a storm, check the attic, the air conditioning closet, and rooms under roof valleys first. A musty smell, soft or stained drywall, or allergy-like symptoms that get worse at home are real signs even when nothing is visible, because mold often grows inside wall cavities and under flooring where you cannot see it.
